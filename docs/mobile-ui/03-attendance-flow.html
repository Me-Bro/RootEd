What I'd actually ship: flow 7 first, then flow 5 wrapping flow 1

Launch with flow 7. It is the plain class list with Present / Absent on every row: nothing to teach, nothing assumed, buildable in days, and every teacher recognises it on sight. Everything below is then a speed-up layered on that same list, never a replacement — a teacher who never discovers any of it still finishes their roll. Flow 5 gets the teacher into the right roster with zero questions (it is the only one that serves a subject teacher's eight-period day). Flow 1 is what she lands on: everyone present, one tap per exception, undo instead of confirm, queued writes. Flow 3 is one tap away for teachers who prefer to check the whole room against one screen. Flow 4's "all present?" question becomes the shortcut on the class teacher's morning card, and flow 2 stays available for schools whose policy requires a spoken roll call. Flow 6 ships in parallel, not instead — the office and the exam coordinator get the desktop console, because chasing 40 sections and marking on behalf of absent staff is a keyboard job, and it writes through the same API with an on-behalf label.
